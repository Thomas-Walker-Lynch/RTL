Dear Sarah,

I am happy, relieved?, to have found your book. I wrote a book on corruption in Austin Texas. You reflect from afar from institutional positions.  Your book is written artfully, reviewed by many, gives high level historical examples, builds a theory, and has the support of your in group. In contrast, I was as though clubbed over the head in the park and robbed. It was personal and completely unexpected. My book is compilation of documents using a singular event as an example, and reading it is a long slog intended for those who I imagined would care what was happening, though such imagined people as it turned out, did not exist. Then I stood alone against the blow-back. 

In writing you I reflect upon Michael Servetus, who sent his marked up copy of "Institutes of the Christian Religion back to John Calvin in the spirit of intellectual alliance", and was subsequently burned at the stake upon meeting Calvin in person. I can only hope your concern over corruption is sincere rather than a political tool, as I too have marked up "On Corruption in America and What is at Stake". Surely a clear understanding is a requisite for establishing solutions, so with the benefit of having read your book, and my own experience, I have some suggestions.

"The Myth of the Peaceful Savage", followed by re-examination and discovery of new sites has thoroughly discounted the Rousseau's, and the Bible's, view of a paradise lost. This is the 'halo error' of psychology read back into history. 380,000 years of the inability to count past 3, culture based on visions hallucinogenic mushroom trips, no idea of what the germs or the stars are, is nothing to celebrate. No limits in war, the torture of captured prisoners, the wiping out of complete other tribes, are reprehensible act. Consider early attempts at inter-tribal "radio" communication, where capturing an enemy and driving arrows through them served as a mechanism to send screaming messages to ancestors. The archaeological record reveals mass death pits in Neolithic Europe, such as those at Talheim. As for being one with the environment, the archaeological record strongly points to the extinction of Pleistocene megafauna at early human hands. That they did not deplete fisheries nor clear forests was due to lack of technology, not will.

Ancient Greece provides stark examples of greed. Alcibiades constantly shifted allegiances between Athens, Sparta, and Persia purely for personal wealth and survival. The vaunted Athenian democratic ideal enthusiastically utilized its sense of equality to vote for the execution of Socrates when his intellectual rigor became politically inconvenient. What you describe around the sacrifice celebrations is what sociologists call pseudo community and it is still found today in analogous social events.

In the modern American era, the omission of Tammany Hall, the Chicago Machine, and localized government fraud strongly suggests a selective narrative. True corruption is prevalent far beyond investment banking and defense contractors. In my book, ITIO a Child, I documented how social workers and lawyers weaponize Violence Against Women Act (VAWA) funds, shellacking fathers within the family court system. This is where corruption ceases to be theoretical and becomes measurable in driven suicide rates and generational trauma. I wonder if you had written about these things, how your in-group would have responded. Would the book be as celebrated?

As for a foundation, I suggest three forces, the being an unconventional, but not contradictory, interpretation of the Milgram Experiment. The person at the controls is compliant to authority because he or she feels part of the in-group. The person at the controls does not know the person in the electric chair, and the person at the chair is described in terms that makes him or her a part of the out-group. You had a section in your book where you started to discuss in-group and out-group dynamics, and I would suggest this would be a better description of a fundamental force involved, one truly rooted in evolution and survival of our species. Humans have large brains because they replaced nature with surviving in the presence of each other. We all have the same DNA, and evolution pervades the population and history.

The second being a variation on the theory put forth in "Secular Cycles". That there is a sociopathy cycle in our society. You touched on this when noting that after a catastrophe that people become more caring. Turchin applied Marxist and Malthusian philosophy to distinct population groups. In his first book on the subject, rural and urban. He described how population increase among rural people forces the family to make tough decisions, and send their sons to the cities, where most will die. The surplus of brain power to choose among then improves the skilled crafts, enriching craft shop owners. The increased population then drives up property value, which enriches the elite class. Everyone acts differently. I would propose in lock step with this, that people become steeled and uncaring at the very times that this is needed for drive evolutionary forces and to keep the cycle going.

For the third force consider these two events: A) On Flight 148 into Strasbourg, a pilot selected the incorrect of two settings on a mode switch. This sent the A380 into the side of a mountain and all aboard died. B) In a grocery story in Philadelphia, grocery story clerk Timmy set the toggle break on the wrong wheel of a grocery car. The card then swung right and knocked some canned tomatoes off of a shelf. It is the nature of their positions that CEOs and people making high level economic decisions can cause immense damage, but that doesn't necessarily mean that the person was being more selfish than the average person. 

We all have the same DNA, and have for all of history. There is a saying among lawyers, "The problem with lawyers is that they are no more honest than everyone else." I wonder how many of the kleptocrats in your book were 'just doing their job'. 

In 1997 fund manager Andre Lee loaned 265 million dollars on company books to a taxi company in Indonesia. The taxi company converted the money to Indonesian currency to use it. Lee was planning to break that risk up and sell it as bonds to investors. That was the nature of their business. Before he he could do that, the Indonesian currency lost value, and the taxi company could not make its USD loan payments because they had Indonesian currency had become worthless. That caused the Peregrine to collapse, followed by a cascade of failures that took down the entire Asian economy.

People were talking about the long cycles and Malthusian forces and what to do about them long before Turchin wrote "Secular Cycles". The movement to artificially curb population growth was discussed in the 1950s, with the likes of Konrad Lorenz commenting on proposals, in one case he said that homosexuality was not the right solution. Whether by intention or not, population decline is happening in much of the world. However, not everyone got the memo, and now poor high population areas are coming into equilibrium with lower population areas. For those who remember it, the debate over these population movements feel like those before WWII when the Jews wanted out of Germany, were even asked to leave by the government, but other nations would not accept them. Similarly for the gypsies, and then people in Eastern Europe when the red tide rolled over. (I think there were better ways to handle this situation, opening the border was not right, control of the border is important, but at the same time I think we are missing out on an opportunity to facilitate many people in finding a better life.)

An fund manager given insured money is going to make high risk investments with it. I would suggest then, that the law you need is one that prevents Congress from insuring investment funds without control of what those funds are to be invested in, or at least, payment for that insurance commensurate with the risk. 

Your section detailing the farmer's collective teaching other farmers was insightful. Distributed, peer-to-peer knowledge transfer is vastly superior to top-down hierarchical control. This looks like an excellent approach for our times. Or is it? We are in the part of the sociopathy cycle where people are not receptive.  Congress.gov, GovTrack.us, LegiScan sort of provide this information.

There is an Aluminum factory in Bettendorf Iowa that is so long that a person standing at one end, can see the horizon in the building. Ingots of Aluminum are moved around by overhead cranes as though they weigh no more than a cord of wood. A person could try to push one of them with a pickup truck, and not be able to move it. Upstairs in the control room, a person can twist a knob to make them move. The power of the fingers moves all that mass.

I suggest that some simple actions, some new products, could profoundly change the stability and direction of our future. I am working on two currently.  One is called "Reasoning Technology", the other one I haven't named yet, but it helps people trade goods. They would fundamentally change how people make decisions and how they conduct business. There is more yet to be added to this portfolio, but this is the start of it.
